Run Your Shop Like a Business · Day 5 of 5

Making fixes stick

About a 5-minute read, plus a short worksheet

Most owners fix a leak in a good month. Then it comes back in a busy one. This last lesson is about why that happens and how the most profitable shops keep their gains.

Why leaks come back

A fix that lives in your head lasts until you get busy. You raise prices on new quotes, then forget during the spring rush. You start sending confirmations, then stop the first week you're short a tech. Nobody decided to stop. It just wasn't anyone's job.

Written down, owned, checked

A fix sticks when three things are true:

  • It's written down. What exactly happens, in a sentence anyone could follow.
  • Someone owns it. One name, not "the office" or "we."
  • Someone checks it. A number you look at every week that tells you it's still happening.

Let the system remember

The fixes that last longest are the ones nobody has to remember: confirmation texts that send themselves, review requests that go out after every job, invoice reminders on a schedule. If it repeats, automate it, so a bad week doesn't undo it.

Reviews are a lead source

Reviews aren't a vanity number. They're where a lot of your calls come from. Shops that ask after every job, make it one tap, and reply to every review (good and bad) get more calls than shops that do great work quietly.

What profitable shops have in common

  1. They know their numbers, including a fair wage for the owner.
  2. They price from their own costs, not the competition's.
  3. They confirm every appointment.
  4. They invoice the same day and follow up on a schedule.
  5. They write their processes down.

You now know where to look. What you don't know yet is your number, which leak to fix first, and in what order. That part is different for every shop.
