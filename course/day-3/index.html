Run Your Shop Like a Business · Day 3 of 5

Scheduling, no-shows and drive time

About a 5-minute read, plus a short worksheet

A customer who isn't home doesn't just cost you the job. It costs the whole trip. Today is about what empty slots and zig-zag days really cost, and why the fix is a system instead of more effort.

What a no-show really costs

When a customer isn't home or cancels last minute, you lose more than the ticket:

  • A slot you usually can't fill that late
  • Fuel and drive time there and back
  • A tech you're still paying for those hours
  • Often a second trip later to do the same job

One no-show a week sounds small. Fifty a year, times your average ticket, usually isn't.

The zig-zag day

A schedule built in the order calls came in sends trucks back and forth across town. Nobody notices, because everyone was busy all day. But the drive time adds up, and on a bad week it can eat one or two jobs you could have fit in.

Grouping jobs by area, even loosely, gives that time back.

Confirmations work, if they always happen

Most no-shows aren't people blowing you off. They forgot. A simple reminder fixes most of it:

  • A text the day before, with the arrival window
  • A text when the tech is on the way
  • An easy way to reschedule instead of just not being there

The catch: doing this by hand works for about two weeks. Then a slammed Monday hits, the texts don't go out, and the no-shows come back. That's why the real fix is a system that sends them every time, whether or not anyone remembers.
