What does your tech really cost per hour?

The 2027 math most trades owners have never done, step by step, with a calculator so you can run it with your own numbers.

By Jen Voiselle · Compass Business Solutions

Most owners price off a number that felt right a few years ago. The tech makes $30 an hour, the rate is $95, so it feels like there's $65 of room in every hour.

There isn't. Here's why, using one simple example. Your numbers will be different. That's the point of the calculator further down.

Step 1: What a paid hour really costs

Start with everything it costs you to have that tech on the clock for one hour. (Example numbers, rounded.)

Wage$30.00
Payroll taxes (Social Security, Medicare, unemployment), about 10%$3.00
Workers' comp, about 5% of wages (yours depends on your trade and state)$1.50
Health insurance and benefits, $600/month$3.46
Truck: payment, insurance, fuel, maintenance, $1,500/month$8.65
Tools, phone, uniforms, $150/month$0.87
Real cost of one paid hour$47.48

Monthly costs are spread over about 173 paid hours a month (40 hours a week).

Step 2: Not every paid hour gets billed

Drive time. Parts runs. Callbacks you don't charge for. Shop time, meetings, waiting on a customer. You pay for all of it, but none of it shows up on an invoice.

Say 70% of paid hours end up billed. Then every billable hour has to carry the cost of the unbilled ones:

Real cost of one paid hour$47.48
÷ share of hours you bill70%
Real cost per billable hour$67.83

Step 3: What's actually left from $95

At a $95 rate, you're not keeping $65 an hour. You're keeping about:

$27

And that's before the office, software, insurance, the owner's own pay and any profit. For a lot of shops, that $27 disappears fast. It's why so many busy trades businesses keep only 2 or 3 cents of every dollar, when industry benchmarks say closer to 20 cents is doable.

What to do with your number

  • If the gap is thin, your rate is the first thing to look at. New quotes can get a new rate right away. Current customers get notice.
  • If your billable share is low, look at routing, parts runs and callbacks before you look at your techs. It's usually the process, not the people.
  • If the truck line surprised you, you're not alone. It's the cost most owners forget to count per hour.
