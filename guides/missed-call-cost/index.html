How much does a missed call cost your business?

The simple math on what unanswered calls really cost, step by step, with a calculator so you can run it with your own numbers.

By Jen Voiselle · Compass Business Solutions

Here's the short answer: a missed call costs you your average job, times the chance that caller would have booked, times your margin. In the example below, that works out to about $90 of profit every time the phone rings out. Six missed calls a week turns into about $27,000 a year.

Nobody misses calls on purpose. You're under a sink, on a roof, or driving 3A with both hands on the wheel. The phone rings, you think "I'll call them back," and by the time you do, they've already booked someone else. So let's put a number on it, because a number is a lot easier to fix than a feeling.

Step 1: Count your missed calls

Don't guess. Open your phone's call log (or your business line's call history) and count the calls you didn't answer last week. Skip the spam and the people you know. Count the numbers you didn't recognize.

Don't count voicemails instead. Someone with water on the floor or no heat in January usually doesn't leave a message. They just call the next name on the list.

For our example, say it's 6 missed calls a week. (Example number. Yours will be different.)

Step 2: How many would have booked?

Not every caller is a job. Some are price shoppers, some are out of your area, some want something you don't do. Look at the calls you do answer: out of 10 new callers, how many end up on the schedule? That's your booking rate.

We'll use 50%, so half of those 6 callers would have turned into work. (Example.)

Step 3: Do the missed call math

Now put a dollar amount on it. You need your average job (total sales divided by number of jobs) and your gross margin (what's left after the tech's time and materials). (Example numbers.)

Missed calls a week6
× 50% that would have booked3 jobs
× $450 average job$1,350 a week
× 50 working weeks$67,500 a year
Profit lost at a 40% gross margin$27,000 a year

Divide that back out and each missed call is worth about $225 in sales and $90 in profit. That's not counting the customer who would have called you back every year for the next ten years, or told their neighbor about you.

Why use margin and not the full job? Because if you'd done that job, you'd also have paid for the tech's time and the parts. The profit is the part that actually walked away.

Step 4: How much a fast callback wins back

You can't answer every call. Nobody can. But you can call back fast, and a caller who hears from you in 10 minutes is a lot more likely to still be shopping than one who hears from you at 8 o'clock that night.

Say a quick callback (or an automatic text that says "On a job, I'll call you in 15 minutes") wins back 30% of those lost jobs. (Example. Track your own for a month and you'll know your number.)

Jobs lost a week3
× 30% won back0.9 jobs a week
× $450 × 50 weeks$20,250 a year
Profit saved at 40% margin$8,100 a year

What to do with your number

  • Set callback times. Check missed calls at every stop: before you walk in, when you walk out, and at lunch. Call or text back right then, not "later."
  • Turn on a text-back. Most business phone apps and many carriers can send an automatic text when you miss a call. Keep it short: who you are, that you're on a job, and when you'll call.
  • Look at when you miss them. If most missed calls land between 8 and 10 in the morning, that's when someone needs to be on the phone, whether it's a spouse, an office person, or a service.
  • Make the most of the ones you catch. A call is only worth something if the price is right. If you haven't checked your service call fee lately, start there.

Questions owners ask

How do you figure out what a missed call costs?
Multiply your missed calls by the share that would have turned into a job, then by your average job size. That's the revenue you lost. Multiply that by your gross margin to see the profit you lost, and by about 50 working weeks to see the yearly number.

Do people leave a voicemail when they call a contractor?
Some do, many don't. Someone with water on the floor or no heat usually just calls the next name on the list. That's why your phone's missed call log is a better count than your voicemail box.

What's the cheapest way to stop losing jobs to missed calls?
Start with a fast callback habit: check missed calls at set times and call or text back within minutes, not hours. An automatic text-back when you can't pick up helps too. Then look at who covers the phone during your busiest hours.
